If you were expecting to collect a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) card when you land in the UK, that has changed. The UK has moved to eVisas: a digital record of your immigration status that lives in an online account.

## What is an eVisa?

An eVisa is a digital record of your identity, your immigration status (for example, Student visa) and its conditions (for example, whether you can work and for how many hours). You get one when your UK visa application is approved. According to GOV.UK, eVisas have replaced physical immigration documents such as the BRP.

> **Good to know:** setting up your account and viewing your eVisa is **free**. Nobody should charge you to "activate" or "unlock" it.

## Step 1: Set up your UKVI account

You need a UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) account to see your eVisa. When your visa is approved, your decision email explains how to create the account. Use the same identity document (usually your passport) that you used in your visa application, so your eVisa can be linked to you.

The eVisa is usually available straight away once it is linked to your account, but GOV.UK says it can take up to 24 hours.

Start here: [GOV.UK: eVisas, access and use your online immigration status](https://www.gov.uk/evisa).

## Step 2: Make sure your passport details are in the account

Your eVisa is checked by airlines and UK Border Force through your passport. Before you travel, make sure the passport you will travel on is added to your UKVI account. **If you renew your passport, update it in your account before your next trip**, otherwise the airline may not be able to see your permission to enter the UK.

## Step 3: Prove your status with a share code

Once you are in the UK, you will be asked to prove your immigration status, for example by your university at registration, a landlord, or an employer if your visa allows work. You do this by generating a **share code** in your UKVI account and giving it to them, along with your date of birth. They use it to view your status online.

- Each share code is for a specific purpose (for example, right to work or right to rent), so choose the right one.
- Share codes expire, so generate a new one when you need it.

## Step 4: Keep your details up to date

Update your UKVI account if you change your passport, phone number, email or address. If something on your eVisa is wrong (for example your name, date of birth or the visa conditions), report it through the GOV.UK eVisa pages straight away rather than waiting until you travel.

## What if I still have a visa sticker in my passport?

GOV.UK says a visa vignette (sticker) stays valid until it expires. Follow the instructions in your decision letter and make sure you also have your UKVI account set up, because your long-term status is held as an eVisa.

## Quick checklist before you fly

1. UKVI account created and you can log in.
2. Your eVisa shows the correct name, dates and conditions.
3. The passport you are travelling on is linked to the account.
4. Your CAS, offer letter and accommodation details are saved on your phone and printed.
5. You know how to generate a share code for your university registration.
