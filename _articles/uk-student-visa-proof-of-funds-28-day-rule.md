Most Student visa refusals we review are not about the course or the university. They are about money: the wrong amount, held for the wrong length of time, shown on the wrong kind of statement.

## 1. How much money you need

For a UK Student visa you must show two things:

- **Your course fees** for one academic year (up to 9 months). The amount is on your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS). If you have already paid part of it, the CAS should show that, and you only need to show what is still outstanding.
- **Money to live on** (the "financial requirement"), for up to 9 months.

| Where you will study | Per month | For 9 months |
|---|---|---|
| London (City of London and the 32 boroughs) | £1,529 | £13,761 |
| Outside London | £1,171 | £10,539 |

These are the figures on GOV.UK at the time of writing. They change from time to time, so always check the [official Student visa money page](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money) on the day you prepare your documents. If you are bringing a partner or children, you need extra money for each of them.

> **Example:** You are studying outside London. Your first-year tuition is £15,000 and you have paid a £5,000 deposit. You need to show £10,000 (remaining fees) + £10,539 (living costs) = **£20,539**. Our [UK Student Visa Financial Calculator](../#calculator) works this out for you, including the Naira equivalent.

## 2. The 28-day rule

The money must have been in the account for **at least 28 days in a row**, and the last day of that 28-day period must be **within 31 days of the date you apply**.

GOV.UK's own example: if you apply on 1 January, you must show the funds were in the account for at least the 28-day period ending on 1 December or later.

"In a row" is the part people miss. If the balance drops below the required amount on even one day, for example because of a transfer or a bank charge, the 28 days start again from the day after the balance went back up.

## 3. Do Nigerian applicants need to show this?

Yes. Some nationalities on the "differential evidence" list do not have to submit financial documents up front. Nigeria is not on that list, so Nigerian applicants must submit full evidence. You are also exempt if you have already lived in the UK with a valid visa for at least 12 months when you apply.

## 4. Whose account can the money be in?

- **Your own account**, in your name.
- **A parent's or legal guardian's account.** You will also need proof of the relationship (usually your birth certificate) and a letter from your parent or guardian confirming they consent to the money being used for your studies.
- **An official financial sponsor or student loan.** You need a letter from the sponsor or loan company.

Money in an uncle's, a friend's or a business account does not meet the rules on its own.

## 5. Mistakes that get applications refused

1. **The balance dips during the 28 days.** Check every single day on the statement, not just the opening and closing balance.
2. **The statement is too old.** The 28-day period must end within 31 days of your application date. A statement printed two months ago will not work.
3. **Missing details.** The statement must show your (or your parent's) name, the account number, the bank's name and logo, the dates covered and the balance throughout.
4. **The wrong type of money.** Shares, bonds, pension funds and money you cannot withdraw immediately are not accepted.
5. **Exchange rate surprises.** UKVI converts Naira to pounds using the OANDA rate on the date you apply. If the Naira weakens, a balance that was enough last month may fall short. Keep a buffer.
6. **Unexplained large deposits.** Lump sums that arrive just before the 28 days start can raise questions about where the money came from. Be ready to explain them.

> **Never use borrowed "show money" or altered statements.** UKVI verifies bank statements with banks. False documents lead to refusal and can lead to a ban from the UK of up to 10 years. Fyzzo Voyage does not create, alter or supply financial documents.

## 6. A simple timeline

- **8 to 10 weeks before you apply:** work out your exact total using your CAS and our calculator, then add a buffer for exchange-rate changes.
- **At least 28 days before you apply:** the full amount should be in the account and must not drop below it.
- **Within 31 days of your application:** get the final statement or bank letter covering the full 28 days.
